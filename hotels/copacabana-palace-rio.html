Copacabana Palace: Rio's film-star hotel since 1923

🏖️ Rio de Janeiro, Brazil · 🎬 Flying Down to Rio · 1930s

The white Copacabana Palace has been Rio de Janeiro's most famous hotel since 1923. Hollywood put it on screen in 1933, and film stars have been checking in ever since.

On screen: Flying Down to Rio

Flying Down to Rio (1933) is credited with putting the hotel on the map. The film reimagines it as the Atlantic Hotel, and features chorus girls dancing on the wings of an airplane flying over Copacabana.

Fun facts

  • The hotel was built by the Guinle family and opened in August 1923.
  • Orson Welles stayed for about eight months in 1942 while making the unfinished film It's All True.
  • The hotel celebrated its 100th anniversary in 2023.
  • Its pool and white façade have been a backdrop for film stars for a century.
  • Accounts of Welles's stay differ in detail, but the stories usually involve a suite emptied into the pool after a breakup.

Visiting today

Rio is warmest from December to March, and Carnival season books out well ahead. Rooms facing the pool and beach are the most sought after, and the hotel sits right on Avenida Atlântica.
