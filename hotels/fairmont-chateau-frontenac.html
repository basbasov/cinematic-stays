Château Frontenac: the Quebec City hotel from Hitchcock's I Confess

🏰 Quebec City, Canada · 🎬 I Confess · 1950s

The copper-roofed Château Frontenac dominates Quebec City's skyline, and Alfred Hitchcock put it on screen in I Confess (1953). It is a working hotel, and you can sleep inside the landmark.

On screen: I Confess

Hitchcock filmed I Confess in Hollywood and Quebec in the summer and autumn of 1952, with Montgomery Clift and Anne Baxter. In the plot, the killer flees into the nearby Château Frontenac hotel, and the hotel's real manager of the time, George Jessop, plays himself in the film.

Fun facts

  • Canadian Pacific built the hotel in 1892 and 1893, designed by architect Bruce Price in the château style.
  • It is named after Louis de Buade, comte de Frontenac, a governor of New France.
  • The central tower was added in 1926 and changed the hotel's silhouette.
  • It hosted the Québec Conferences of 1943 and 1944 with Roosevelt, Churchill and Mackenzie King.
  • It was designated a National Historic Site of Canada in 1981.

Visiting today

The Dufferin Terrace outside is a good spot for views over the St. Lawrence River. Book river-view rooms early in summer and during the winter carnival.
