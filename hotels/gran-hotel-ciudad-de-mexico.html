Gran Hotel Ciudad de México: the stained-glass hotel from Spectre

🌮 Mexico City, Mexico · 🎬 Spectre · 2010s

Under a Tiffany-style stained-glass ceiling in Mexico City's historic centre, the Gran Hotel is one of the city's most striking interiors. James Bond has been here twice.

On screen: Spectre

The hotel appears in the James Bond films Spectre (2015) and License to Kill (1989). Its cage-style elevators and lobby feature in Spectre's Mexico City sequence, although the street entrance was shot at another building.

Fun facts

  • The building was inaugurated in 1899 as a department store, in the Art Nouveau style.
  • It began operating as a hotel in 1968, just before the Mexican Olympics.
  • Its glass ceiling was reportedly imported from France in 1908.
  • The lobby has one of the first panoramic elevators installed in Mexico City.
  • Its building sits among other Art Nouveau landmarks of the Mexican capital, such as the Palacio de Bellas Artes.

Visiting today

The hotel is in the historic centre, a short walk from the Zócalo and the city's major landmarks. The lobby is open to look at, so it is worth a visit even for a quick coffee, and the stained-glass ceiling is best seen from the ground floor.
