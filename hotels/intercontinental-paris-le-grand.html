InterContinental Paris Le Grand: the hotel from Alphaville and Frantic

🗼 Paris, France · 🎬 Alphaville, Frantic · 1980s

Standing beside the Opéra Garnier with the Café de la Paix at its corner, Le Grand Hôtel has been a Paris landmark since 1862. Two very different films, one French New Wave and one Hollywood thriller, used it as a location.

On screen: Alphaville

Jean-Luc Godard filmed large portions of Alphaville (1965) here, using the driveway, lobby, elevators, hallways and winter garden. The film's opening shows its hero checking in and riding up to his room, shot with the help of two glass-walled elevators that stand side by side. Roman Polanski set much of Frantic (1988) at the hotel too. Filming took place in Paris in 1987, with exteriors shot outside the building on Rue Scribe, while most of the interiors were built on soundstages.

Fun facts

  • The Pereire brothers built the hotel, and architect Alfred Armand designed it in the Haussmann style.
  • Empress Eugénie inaugurated it on 5 May 1862, and it opened to guests on 30 June that year.
  • Its Café de la Paix has been on the ground floor since the hotel opened.
  • It closed in December 2001 for a major renovation and reopened in April 2003 under its current name.

Visiting today

The hotel sits on Rue Scribe next to the Opéra and the Opéra metro station, so it is a handy base for the grands boulevards and the Louvre. Even if you do not stay, a coffee at the Café de la Paix lets you see the building from the outside.
