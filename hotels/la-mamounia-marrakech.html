La Mamounia: Marrakech's palace hotel from Hitchcock's thriller

🕌 Marrakech, Morocco · 🎬 The Man Who Knew Too Much · 1950s

Set among orange and olive gardens at the edge of the old city, La Mamounia has been Marrakech's grandest address since 1923. Alfred Hitchcock filmed here for The Man Who Knew Too Much.

On screen: The Man Who Knew Too Much

Hitchcock shot scenes for his 1956 thriller The Man Who Knew Too Much, starring James Stewart and Doris Day, at the hotel, including its lobby and gardens. The Moroccan setting is central to the film's opening act.

Fun facts

  • La Mamounia opened in 1923, when Morocco was a French protectorate.
  • Its name comes from the gardens it stands in, once called Arset Al Mamoun.
  • Winston Churchill visited nearly a dozen times and often painted the gardens from his usual rooms.
  • In 1943 Churchill invited Franklin D. Roosevelt to Marrakech after the Casablanca Conference.
  • Guests over the years have included Charlie Chaplin, Yves Saint Laurent and Paul McCartney.

Visiting today

The gardens are a highlight even if you only stop for tea. Marrakech is hottest in July and August, so spring and autumn are the pleasant seasons.
