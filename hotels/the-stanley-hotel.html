The Stanley Hotel: the Colorado hotel behind The Shining

❄️ Estes Park, USA · 🎬 The Shining (1997 miniseries), Dumb and Dumber · 1990s

Perched above Estes Park in the Colorado Rockies, the white Stanley Hotel is the place that gave Stephen King the idea for The Shining. It is also a working hotel with some surprising screen credits.

On screen: The Shining (1997 miniseries)

King and his wife stayed here in 1974, as night fell on their drive through Estes Park, and the experience inspired his novel. The hotel later appeared on screen too: it plays the fictional Danbury Hotel in the comedy Dumb and Dumber, and King returned to film the 1997 TV miniseries of The Shining here. Kubrick's 1980 film, by contrast, was not shot at the Stanley.

Fun facts

  • F.O. Stanley, co-inventor of the Stanley Steamer car, opened the hotel in 1909, after moving to Estes Park for his health.
  • Stanley soon sold the hotel to Roe Emery, who also owned the Estes Park Chalets.
  • The hotel and Stanley's own involvement helped the town grow and supported the creation of Rocky Mountain National Park.
  • The hotel is a white Georgian Colonial Revival building on a hill overlooking Estes Park.
  • The Stanley is known for ghost stories, and runs night tours for guests who want them.

Visiting today

Estes Park is the gateway to Rocky Mountain National Park, so summer and early autumn are busiest. Book ahead for October, when horror fans arrive in numbers.
