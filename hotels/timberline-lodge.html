Timberline Lodge: the real Overlook Hotel from The Shining

🏔️ Oregon, USA · 🎬 The Shining · 1980s

High on Oregon's Mount Hood, Timberline Lodge looks every bit the snowbound hotel of your nightmares. It is also a warm, welcoming place to stay, and it is the building Stanley Kubrick used for the Overlook Hotel's exterior in The Shining (1980).

On screen: The Shining

Kubrick filmed the lodge's exterior and establishing shots here, but the famous interiors, from the Gold Room to the endless corridors, were built on sound stages at Elstree Studios in England. So the hotel you see from outside is real, while the rooms inside the film are not.

Fun facts

  • Timberline Lodge was built in the 1930s as a public works project, with handcrafted timber, stonework and ironwork.
  • President Franklin D. Roosevelt dedicated the lodge in 1937.
  • In Stephen King's novel the haunted room is 217. The lodge asked for a change so guests wouldn't avoid its real Room 217, and the film used 237 instead. Timberline has no Room 237.
  • King's novel was inspired by a stay at a different hotel, the Stanley Hotel in Colorado.

Visiting today

The lodge is open year-round and sits at the base of a ski area, so winter and summer both work. Expect cosy fireplaces and mountain views rather than ghosts.
